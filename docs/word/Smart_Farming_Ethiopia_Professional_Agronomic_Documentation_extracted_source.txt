Abstract 
 
Agriculture remains highly dependent on timely decisions related to crop disease identification, 
farm monitoring, treatment guidance, and environmental observation. In many local farming 
settings, however, digital systems are limited by poor connectivity, fragmented records, low 
digital literacy, inconsistent field guidance, and lack of mobile-first farmer support. This project 
addresses these limitations by designing and implementing the Smart Farm Farmer App, an AI-
assisted, offline-first smart farming system that combines a farmer-centered Flutter mobile 
application, a Laravel backend API and backoffice, and a MySQL database. 
 
The system supports farm, plot, and planting management; disease scan and reporting 
workflows; disease history; soil health monitoring; weather monitoring; alerts; localization; and 
offline synchronization. The mobile application is intentionally designed for practical field use, 
with simplified interaction flows, larger text hierarchy, reduced screen noise, and resilience 
under unstable connectivity. The disease workflow supports offline and online inference-related 
operations, evidence image capture, disease reporting, confirmation-aware treatment guidance, 
and record synchronization with the backend. The backend provides authentication, API 
resources, structured storage, policy-based access control, and data persistence for farmer 
activities and review-aware workflows. 
The implementation demonstrates how software engineering principles such as modular 
architecture, role-aware backend policy enforcement, offline-first design, synchronization, error 
handling, localization, and secure API integration can be applied to a real agricultural system. 
The project contributes a practical smart farming platform tailored for real-world farmer 
usability while remaining extensible for future production improvements such as stronger model 
governance, richer expert workflows, and broader agricultural analytics.

Chapter One: Introduction 
1.1 Background of the Study 
Agriculture increasingly depends on accurate, timely, and context-aware digital information. 
Farmers need practical assistance in managing farms, monitoring crop health, detecting diseases 
early, recording evidence, observing environmental conditions, and acting on guidance before 
problems become severe. In many field conditions, however, software systems fail because they 
assume continuous internet connectivity, high digital literacy, or workstation-based access. 
These assumptions do not match many real-world agricultural environments. 
 
The Smart Farm Farmer App was developed to address these practical gaps through a mobile-
first and offline-first architecture. The system integrates a Flutter-based farmer application, a 
Laravel backend API and administrative backoffice, and a MySQL database. The 
implementation supports real farm workflows including farm registration, plot management, 
planting records, disease scan operations, disease history, evidence-supported reporting, soil 
health monitoring, weather monitoring, alerts, and farmer-oriented advisory access. 
 
Unlike generic agricultural record systems, this project specifically emphasizes usability for 
actual farmers. The mobile interface is simplified to reduce cognitive load, support unstable 
connectivity, and improve readability. The system also incorporates structured disease-related 
workflows that acknowledge the operational importance of evidence images, treatment guidance 
safety, offline capture, and synchronization.

1.2 Statement of the Problem 
 
In many farming environments, operational challenges include: 
 
• fragmented or paper-based farm records 
• late identification of crop disease symptoms 
• weak continuity between field capture and central reporting 
• poor connectivity that interrupts digital workflows 
• lack of mobile systems designed for farmer usability 
• difficulty accessing practical treatment or monitoring guidance 
• limited integration between farm records, disease history, weather context, and alerting 
mechanisms 
 
These gaps reduce timely decision-making and make it difficult for farmers and support 
personnel to act on reliable, traceable information.

Chapter Two: Review of Related Literature and Existing Systems 
2.1 Introduction 
This chapter reviews relevant concepts, prior system approaches, and gaps that justify the 
development of the Smart Farm Farmer App. 
2.2 Concept of Smart Farming 
Smart farming applies digital technologies to improve decision-making in agricultural 
production. Typical capabilities include crop monitoring, environmental observation, record 
management, prediction, disease recognition, alerts, and advisory support. Effective smart 
farming systems must translate technical inputs into usable field actions. 
2.3 AI in Agriculture 
Artificial intelligence in agriculture is commonly used for disease identification, yield 
estimation, pattern recognition, irrigation support, and anomaly detection. However, usefulness 
depends not only on model output but also on workflow context, evidence quality, user trust, and 
safe action guidance. 
2.4 Mobile-Based Agricultural Decision Support 
Mobile applications are critical for agriculture because they bring data entry, observation, and 
feedback directly to the field. However, many systems are limited by overly complex interfaces, 
lack of offline support, or weak alignment with farmer realities. 
 
 
2.5 Offline-First Systems in Rural Environments 
Offline-first design is especially important in agricultural environments where internet access is 
unstable, intermittent, or unavailable at the point of use. A system that loses its usefulness 
whenever connectivity drops is not operationally reliable for fieldwork.

2.6 Agricultural Disease Detection Systems 
Disease detection systems often focus on classification quality alone. In practice, a usable system 
must also support: 
• evidence capture 
• reporting 
• record history 
• confidence-aware interpretation 
• synchronization 
• safety-aware guidance 
2.7 Soil Health and Weather Monitoring Systems 
Soil and weather context directly influence crop condition and intervention planning. When these 
modules are disconnected from farmer workflow, the system becomes fragmented. Integrated 
monitoring improves situational awareness.

4.9 Security Design 
 
Security design elements include: 
 
• authenticated API access 
• role-aware server-side authorization 
• token-based session handling 
• protected evidence image access 
• prevention of unauthorized cross-farmer access 
• safe session clearing and offline unlock considerations 
 
4.10 Synchronization and Offline Design 
 
The mobile system stores records locally and replays or merges them with server state when 
connectivity is restored. This is necessary because field capture must not fail when network 
availability is poor. 
4.11 Localization Design 
 
The system uses a centralized localization structure and language state propagation to support 
four local languages. Farmer-facing screens are designed to reduce mixed-language behavior and 
fallback inconsistency.

Chapter Seven: Conclusion and Recommendations 
7.1 Conclusion 
This project designed and implemented a practical smart farming system that integrates a farmer-
focused Flutter mobile app, Laravel backend services, and MySQL data storage into a unified 
agricultural workflow. The system addresses major field-level problems including fragmented 
records, weak digital continuity, delayed disease tracking, and poor connectivity tolerance. By 
supporting offline-first operation, disease-related workflows, farm hierarchy management, 
localized interfaces, and monitoring modules, the project demonstrates the application of 
software engineering principles to real agricultural needs. 
 
The resulting system is not merely a prototype of disconnected screens. It represents a modular 
and extensible platform where usability, synchronization, traceability, and practical field 
operation are treated as first-class engineering concerns. 
7.2 Contributions of the Project 
• farmer-centered mobile smart farming platform 
• offline-first mobile workflow design 
• integrated farm, plot, and planting structure 
• disease report and history workflow with evidence awareness 
• soil health and weather monitoring integration 
• alerts and advisory-oriented interface support 
• multilingual interface support 
• practical backend policy and API structure
